Yes, three columns make sense, and the main win is getting the whole bench into one screen with no page scroll. I'd change one thing in your arrangement: put the timegrapher in the right column above the sliders, not in the centre under the movement.

Why move the timegrapher - Height. The movement drawing is tall and narrow. Stacking the timegrapher under it either shrinks the drawing or brings the scrolling back. - Cause and effect. The loop a learner repeats is "move a slider, read the trace". Putting the sliders directly under the readout they change keeps that loop in one glance. - Space. Each puzzle only shows one to three sliders, so they fit under the instrument without crowding.

Proposed layout on a desktop screen, filling the window exactly:

┌────────────┬──────────────────────┬───────────────────┐
│ Title      │ Movement speed  ▮──  │  Timegrapher      │
│ Calibre    │                      │  trace            │

│            │      Movement        ├───────────────────┤
│ Job card   │      drawing         │  Regulator  ──▮── │

│  goal      │                      │  Position   ▾     │

   ~260px          flexible               ~420px

Other suggestions - Job card in the left rail. The symptom, goal, hold bar and the puzzle 3 question sit under the puzzle picker, so the left column reads top to bottom as "what am I fixing". - Fold onboarding into the left rail. The first-run guide and trace explainer currently sit in a band above everything and push the bench down. That section belongs to the onboarding agent, so it would be a handoff or a joint change. - Phone layout. Stack in the order job, timegrapher, sliders, movement, so puzzle 1 is solvable without scrolling past the drawing. - Short laptop screens. Below about 700px of height the drawing scales down rather than the page scrolling. The left rail scrolls internally if a long explanation opens.
