Short answer: today only two sliders move the drawing, and both are subtle. That's a real gap, not an intended one.

What changes now

  • State of wind shrinks the balance swing (280° to 250° over a day, stop at 42 h). Correct, but you have to be watching the swing's extremes to notice.
  • Beat adjustment shifts the balance's rest point relative to the notch, so the two half-swings differ. Visible only in slow motion.
  • Regulator, timing screws change the period by hundredths of a percent. The physics says the drawing should look identical, and it does.
  • Position drops amplitude 30° in vertical positions. Nothing in the drawing turns.
  • Poising moves nothing at all.

What should change, and what a watchmaker would actually see

  • Regulator index: a lever on the balance cock that swings with the slider, with F and S marks. The adjustment itself is the visual, not its effect on the swing.
  • Beat adjustment: the stud carrier rotates with the slider, and the hairspring's outer attachment moves with it.
  • Timing screws: screws on the rim visibly turn in or out, in pairs.
  • Poising: a mark for the heavy spot (once the player has diagnosed it) and the weight being removed at the chosen rim angle.
  • Position: rotate the whole movement to the chosen orientation with a small gravity arrow, so "crown down" means something.
  • Hairspring: drawn and breathing with the balance. It's the part every other adjustment acts on, and right now it isn't on the page.

All of these are geometry derived from the settings, so they belong in the shapes module in my lane, with the design lane styling them. None changes the physics. The hairspring is the biggest win and the most work; the regulator lever and position rotation are the cheapest and make the most sliders feel alive.

If you want this, I'd do it on a branch as adjustment-visuals, in that order: regulator lever and stud carrier, position rotation, timing screws and poise mark, hairspring last. Each gets named verify-render states.
