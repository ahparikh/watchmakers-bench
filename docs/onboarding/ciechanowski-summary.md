# Ciechanowski's *Mechanical Watch*, summarised for the Bench

Source: https://ciechanow.ski/mechanical-watch/ (Bartosz Ciechanowski, 2022). Read 2026-11-03.
The article explains **how** a watch works, with interactive 3D models. The Bench explains **why it
keeps bad time and how you fix it**, so this summary keeps only what a player needs, and says for
each part which Bench control and puzzle touch it. Section anchors are the article's own headings.

Verified against the article text: sections Power through Keyless Works. The closing sections
(automatic winding, sizes, further reading) were not read in full and are not used here.

## The chain of power, in one paragraph

A coiled **mainspring** stores the energy you put in by winding. A **gear train** carries that
energy to the **escape wheel**, stepping the speed up and the force down on the way. The
**pallet fork** lets the escape wheel advance one tooth at a time, and each release is one
tick. The **balance wheel** and its **hairspring** decide *how often* the fork lets a tooth go,
so they alone set the rate. Everything else (hands, date, crown) is bookkeeping.

## The parts a player needs

| Part | What it does (per the article) | Bench control | Puzzle |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Mainspring and barrel** ([Power](https://ciechanow.ski/mechanical-watch/#power)) | A spiral spring in a drum. Wound tight it pushes hard; as it unwinds it pushes less. Designed for about 40 hours on one wind. | State of wind | 3 Afternoon slump |
| **Gear train** ([Gears](https://ciechanow.ski/mechanical-watch/#gears)) | Four wheels from the barrel to the escape wheel, about 343:1 overall. It turns a slow, strong barrel into a fast, gentle push at the escape wheel so the balance is not pushed "with too much vigor". | none (drawn simplified) | – |
| **Escape wheel and pallet fork** ([Escapement](https://ciechanow.ski/mechanical-watch/#escapement)) | The fork's two ruby stones alternately stop and release the wheel. Each release is a tick and a small push back to the balance, "like giving a push to a person on a swing". | none (watch it on the left) | all |
| **Balance wheel** ([Balance](https://ciechanow.ski/mechanical-watch/#balance)) | The swinging ring. Its swing time depends on its mass distribution and the spring's stiffness; mass further out makes it swing slower. Here, five beats a second (18,000 per hour; the article's watch is 28,800). | Timing screws (inertia) | – |
| **Hairspring** (same section) | A thin spiral spring that pulls the balance back to centre. Stiffer or shorter means quicker swings. The alloy keeps stiffness steady across temperature. | via the regulator | 1 |
| **Regulator** (same section, the "teal components") | Two pins straddle the spring's tail and stop it from flexing freely, which "reduces or increases its effective length". Toward F the active spring is shorter and the watch gains; toward S it is longer and the watch loses. | Regulator index | 1 Runs fast |
| **Stud / beat adjustment** (same section, the "yellow components") | The anchor of the spring's outer end. Turning it rotates the balance's rest position so "both the tick and tock phases" take the same time. | Beat adjustment | 2 Out of beat |
| **Jewels** ([Mainplate](https://ciechanow.ski/mechanical-watch/#mainplate)) | Synthetic ruby bearings with a drop of oil. Friction in them differs when the watch lies flat or stands on edge. | Position | 4 Position error |
| **Crown and keyless works** ([Keyless Works](https://ciechanow.ski/mechanical-watch/#keyless-works)) | Pull the crown to wind, set the date, or set the time. | – | – |
| **Motion works, date** ([Motion Works](https://ciechanow.ski/mechanical-watch/#motion-works), [Date](https://ciechanow.ski/mechanical-watch/#date)) | Gearing that turns one rotation per hour into hands and a date ring. Does not affect rate. | – | – |

## What the article does not cover (the Bench's territory)

The article never names beat error, amplitude, positional error, poise, isochronism, lift angle or
timegraphers. It says how the regulator and the beat adjustment work, but not what a watchmaker
measures to know which one to touch. That gap is the Bench: the timegrapher turns ticks into rate,
amplitude and beat error, and each puzzle is "read the numbers, then touch the right part".

## Numbers the article gives

| Quantity | Value |
| --- | --- |
| Run time on one wind | about 40 hours |
| Barrel turns per wind | close to 7 |
| Gear train ratio, barrel to escape wheel | about 343:1 |
| Balance frequency | 4 swings per second, 8 beats per second, 28,800 beats per hour |

The Bench's first calibre runs at 18,000 beats per hour (5 beats a second), a vintage rate; the
article's is a modern 28,800. The mechanism is the same.

## How the Bench will use this

Each puzzle names the part it is about and links to that part's section as "the manual". The
brief calls this the lab-and-textbook split: the Bench is the lab, the article is the textbook.
